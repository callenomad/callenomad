## ⚡ The 3 Golden Rules of Street Spanish

Before learning a single phrase, internalize these three rules:

### Rule 1: Never translate "Can I have..." literally
In English, "Can I have..." is polite. In Spanish, saying *"¿Puedo tener...?"* sounds bizarre and unnatural (it literally means *"Am I physically capable of possessing...?"*).
* Instead, use:
  * **Mexico / General:** *"¿Me da un café, por favor?"* (Give me...)
  * **Colombia:** *"¿Me regala un café, por favor?"* (Gift me... standard polite form)
  * **Spain:** *"Ponme un café, porfa."* (Put/serve me...)
  * **Argentina:** *"¿Me hacés un café?"* or *"¿Te pido un café?"*

### Rule 2: Soften with "porfa" and natural intonation
Don't overuse rigid, formal politeness when casual warmth will build faster rapport. A quick *"porfa"* (short for *por favor*) with a smile works across all 4 hubs.

### Rule 3: Use local "connective tissue" (filler words)
Textbook learners freeze in dead silence when thinking. Locals use filler sounds that buy time:
* **Mexico:** *"Este...", "O sea...", "Fíjate que..."*
* **Colombia:** *"O sea...", "Pues...", "Entonces..."*
* **Argentina:** *"Mirá...", "O sea...", "Viste que..."*
* **Spain:** *"A ver...", "En plan...", "Es que..."*

## ☕ 1. Cafés, Coworking & Remote Work Survival

| Scenario | What Duolingo Teaches | What Locals Actually Say | Meaning & Context |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Asking for WiFi** | *¿Tiene internet?* | *"Disculpa, ¿cuál es la clave del wifi?"* | "Excuse me, what’s the WiFi password?" |
| **Finding an outlet** | *¿Dónde está la electricidad?* | *"¿Tendrás un enchufe cerca?"* | "Do you have an outlet nearby?" |
| **Asking to share a table** | *¿Esta silla está ocupada?* | *"¿Te molesta si me siento acá?"* | "Mind if I sit here?" |
| **Ordering non-dairy milk** | *Quiero leche de avena.* | *"¿Manejan leche vegetal / de avena?"* | "Do you carry plant/oat milk?" |
| **Asking for the bill quickly** | *La cuenta, por favor.* | *"¿Me cobras, porfa? Pago con tarjeta."* | "Can I settle up? Paying by card." |
| **To go vs. For here** | *Para salir.* | *"Para llevar / Para tomar acá."* | "To go / For here." |

## 📍 4. The 4 Big Nomad Hubs: Local Slang Decoders

### 🇲🇽 Mexico City (CDMX)
*Chilango Spanish is playful, indirect, and packed with double meanings.*

1. **¿Qué onda? / ¿Qué hubo?** — What's up? / How's it going?
2. **Cámara / Ya rugiste** — Deal / Sounds good / You got it.
3. **¿Me da una orden con copia?** — Tacos served with double tortilla (the "copia").
4. **Está chido / Está padre** — That's cool / awesome.
5. **No manches / No mames** — No way! / You're kidding! *(Note: "No manches" is safe everywhere; "No mames" is for close friends).*
6. **Ahorita** — Can mean right now, in 10 minutes, tomorrow, or never. If you need it *now*, say: *"Ahorita mismo"*.

### 🇨🇴 Medellín (Paisa)
*Paisa Spanish is musical, warm, and hyper-polite.*

1. **¿Bien o no? / ¿Bien o qué?** — The quintessential Medellín greeting.
2. **¿Me regala...?** — The universal polite way to ask for anything.
3. **Qué chimba** — How cool / awesome *(very informal)*.
4. **Parce / Parcero** — Bro / mate / friend.
5. **De una** — Absolutely / Let's do it right away.
6. **Dar papaya** — To expose yourself to danger or leave yourself vulnerable ("No des papaya").

### 🇦🇷 Buenos Aires (Porteño)
*Direct, expressive, dramatic, and driven by the Italian-infused voseo.*

1. **Che, ¿cómo andás?** — Hey, how are you doing?
2. **Posta / ¿Es posta?** — For real / Are you serious?
3. **Quilombo** — A total mess / chaos (*"Qué quilombo el tránsito"*).
4. **¿Te va?** — Does that work for you? / Are you up for it?
5. **Re- (prefix)** — Super / extremely (*"Re-lindo"*, *"Re-caro"*, *"Re-tarde"*).
6. **El Voseo Cheat Sheet**:
   * Don't say *tú tienes* ➔ Say **vos tenés**
   * Don't say *tú quieres* ➔ Say **vos querés**
   * Don't say *tú puedes* ➔ Say **vos podés**

### 🇪🇸 Madrid (Castizo)
*Fast, direct, assertive, and conversational.*

1. **¿Tenéis...?** — Do you guys have...? (*vosotros* is standard in Spain).
2. **Mola mazo** — It's super cool / I really like it.
3. **Vale** — Okay / Sure / Got it (used 50+ times a day).
4. **Tío / Tía** — Dude / Mate / Girl.
5. **Flipar** — To be blown away or shocked (*"Estoy flipando con los precios"*).
6. **Tapear / De cañas** — Going out for small beers (*cañas*) and tapas.
